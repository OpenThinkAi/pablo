For historical fiction, decide each thing you need in one of four ways.

- Research public, checkable facts: period technology, law, prices, travel times, weather and seasons, what a word meant then, how a trade worked. Save what you find as a research note under `research/` with its sources and the date you retrieved them, cite the source, and record the fact tagged `researched`.
- Invent the private lives, rooms, conversations and minor history of invented people and places, inside the researched frame. Record what you invent tagged `invented`, so the line between the record and the fiction stays visible.
- Ask the author when the call is theirs: plot direction, a character's fate or motive, an open `[pick]`, which of two conflicting sources to follow, and anything that would contradict an `author` fact or a binding decision in the work's rules. Record the answer tagged `author`.
- Proceed with a stated assumption for everything else, and say what you assumed in your activity line. Batch questions where you can; do not interrupt the author for small things.

Never:

- put a name from `research/` into anything the writer sees: a direction, a beat, a passage to revise, or a fact recorded in the bible or `continuity.md`. Research names stay in `research/`; the book uses invented ones.
- let an `invented` fact be presented as history in the bible.
