# Prose: the shared guide

These rules hold across every work. Examples marked "Flagged" are lines a model
produced and the author rejected.

## Concrete over abstract

Name the thing. Not "a boat" but a thirty-foot lobster boat with a patched wheelhouse.
Not "bad weather" but sleet that freezes to the rope.

Flagged: "The scene was rich with the atmosphere of the working harbor."

## Dialogue is underplayed

People in a scene share context the reader does not have, and they talk like it.
Nobody explains the joke, states the theme, or narrates their feelings.

Flagged: "As you know, Sarah, our family has run this farm for three generations."

## Do not restate the brief

The beat and the bible are scaffolding. Do not paraphrase them into the prose. A
character's defining trait is shown by what they do, never summarized.

## Endings do not editorialize

A scene ends on an image, a line, or an action, not on a paragraph that explains what
it meant.

Flagged: "It was a careful dance they all performed, a quiet agreement to look the other way."

## No foreshadowing summaries

No "little did he know", no "it would be years before", no "this was the beginning of".

## Sentences

Vary length. Straight quotes ("like this"), never curly. No em-dashes; use a comma, a
period, or parentheses. Year ranges: "1920 to 1933", not "1920-1933".

## Names

No stock fiction names (Blackwood, Thorne, Vance, Seraphina). Pull names from the time
and place of the story.

## Length

Hit the requested length within about ten percent.
