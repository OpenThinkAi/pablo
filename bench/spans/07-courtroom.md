<<<span
The third day was the day they put the site foreman on.

His name was Denis Hallorhan and he had been the foreman at the Ardmore
Road site for eleven years, which counsel established in the first two
minutes along with his age, his address, and the fact that he had never
in that time had a serious accident on any site he ran. He said all of
this in the flat, patient voice of a man reading a list, and when he was
finished he put his hands on the rail in front of him and left them
there.

Counsel for the plaintiff was a small woman called Ademola who had been
writing for twenty minutes without once looking up, and who now looked
up.

"Mr Hallorhan, who checked the anchor points on the morning of the
seventeenth?"

"I did."

"You personally."

"I check them every morning. It's the first thing I do. I've done it
every morning for eleven years."

She let that sit. She turned a page, and turned it back, and did not
appear to find what she wanted, and asked him whether he had signed the
sheet.

"There's a sheet," he agreed.

"Did you sign it on the seventeenth?"

He said that he would have. He said that it was the practice on his sites
that the sheet was signed at the start of the shift and that he had never
known it not to be. He said that there had been a difficulty that morning
with the delivery of the mesh, which had come at ten past seven instead
of six, and that this had put everyone behind, and he described the
difficulty with the mesh for some time. Nobody stopped him. The judge
wrote something. In the third row of the public gallery a woman in a grey
coat sat with her hands in her lap and did not move at all.

Once, while he was describing the mesh, he took his right hand off the
rail to indicate the width of a bay, and then he put it back, and then he
took the left one off as well and looked at it as though it belonged to
somebody in the row behind him, and put that back too. The stenographer
recorded none of this because none of it was speech, and the transcript
of the third day runs to sixty-one pages in which Denis Hallorhan is
perfectly consistent.

When he had finished, Ademola asked him again whether he had signed the
sheet on the seventeenth, and he said that he would have, and she thanked
him and sat down, and the whole thing had taken nine minutes.
