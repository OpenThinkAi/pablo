<<<span
It was a sad little shop, really, and it had been sad for years. Yusuf
came in at four and put the ovens on and the smell of them warming was
the best part of his day, which was a heartbreaking thing to be true of
anyone. He shaped ninety loaves by hand. His wrists hurt. He never said
so, which was the kind of quiet dignity nobody notices.

At six he unlocked the door and turned the sign and stood a moment
looking out at the wet street, feeling, as he always did, that the day
had already been decided somewhere else.
