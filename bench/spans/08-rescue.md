<<<span
The call came at 15:52 and by 16:10 there were nine of them on the track
above Cwm Idwal with headtorches they did not need yet and would need
badly in about forty minutes.

What they knew was almost nothing, which was normal. A woman had called
from a phone with one bar and had said that her husband had gone up
towards the ridge at about eleven, that he had a red jacket, and that he
had not come back. She had said it twice, the second time more slowly,
because the first time she had been crying too much to be understood, and
in the recording you could hear her deciding to stop crying, which
Bethan, who had listened to a great many of these, thought was the worst
sound in the world.

They went up in two teams. Bethan took the four newest and went by the
long shoulder, which was slower and safer and would put them at eight
hundred metres in an hour. Gwyn took the rest straight up the scree.

At 16:40 the cloud came down to about seven hundred metres, which meant
that everything above that was inside it, which meant the helicopter,
already twenty minutes out from Caernarfon, was going to turn around and
did.

At 17:20 Bethan's radio said, in Gwyn's voice, quite level: "Bethan,
Gwyn. We've got a dog."

She stopped walking. Behind her the four newest stopped too, in a line,
the way people do.

"Say again."

"A dog. Border collie, no collar, about two hundred metres below the
ridge on the north side, and it will not come to us and it will not go
away. It keeps going back up."

Bethan looked at the wall of cloud above her, which was the colour of wet
newspaper and getting darker, and did the thing she had been trained not
to do, which was to feel hopeful.

"Follow the dog," she said.

"That's not procedure."

"Follow the dog, Gwyn."

They found him at 17:55 at nine hundred and forty metres, in the shelter
of a boulder he had not chosen so much as fallen behind, with a broken
ankle and a temperature of thirty-three point one and about ninety
minutes left in him. The dog was lying against his chest and had been for
six hours, and when they lifted him onto the stretcher the dog got up and
walked down beside it and did not need to be called once.
span>>>
