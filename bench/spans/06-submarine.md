# Forty-one hours

<<<span
The boat had been on the bottom for thirty-one hours when Petrov stopped
pretending to sleep and went forward to look at the manifold himself.

There is a particular quality to the air in a submarine that has been
shut down for a day and a half. It is not that it smells bad, though it
does. It is that it has been breathed. Every cubic foot of it has been
through somebody's lungs eleven or twelve times, and the carbon dioxide
scrubbers had been running on the emergency circuit since the first
watch, which meant they were running at about sixty percent, which meant
that the arithmetic everyone had done privately and nobody had said out
loud came to somewhere between eleven and fourteen hours.

The manifold was aft of frame sixty-two, behind a panel that in the
drawings was hinged and in reality had been welded shut in a refit in
Severodvinsk in a year nobody could now agree on. Petrov had been aboard
for four of those years. He put his hand on the weld and found it cold,
which told him nothing, and he was aware that he had come forward mostly
to be seen to have come forward.

Mikhailov was already there with a torch in his teeth and both arms
inside the panel to the elbow.

"It's not the manifold," Mikhailov said, around the torch.

"You don't know that."

"I know the manifold. It's the valve on the port bank and the valve on
the port bank is a casting from 1988 and it has a crack in it you could
read a newspaper through."

Petrov crouched. He was fifty-three and his knees announced it. Inside
the panel, in the wobbling light, he could see the valve body and could
not see any crack at all, and he understood that Mikhailov could not see
one either, and that Mikhailov had decided that a specific and namable
failure was better for the eleven men aft than an unspecified one.

"Then we cross-connect to starboard," Petrov said.

"We cross-connect to starboard and we lose the isolation."

"Yes."

"And if starboard goes, we have nothing at all, rather than half of
something."

"Yes."

Mikhailov took the torch out of his mouth and looked at him for the first
time. Somewhere above them, four hundred and ten feet of black water
away, it was the middle of the afternoon. There would be surface ships by
now. There would be a great deal of activity of a kind that made
excellent television and no difference whatsoever at four hundred and ten
feet.

"All right," Mikhailov said. "Tell me when."
span>>>

The cross-connect took nineteen minutes and both men were wrong about
which valve it was.
